1. The story in one minute

Cadence Sinclair Eastman belongs to a rich family who spend summers on their private island, Beechwood, off Massachusetts. She, her cousins Johnny and Mirren, and Gat (nephew of Aunt Carrie's partner Ed) call themselves the Liars. In summer fifteen Cadence falls in love with Gat. She is then found half-drowned on the beach and can no longer remember what happened. She believes she hit her head while night swimming. She suffers terrible migraines.

Two years later (summer seventeen) she returns for four weeks and slowly remembers: the family was tearing itself apart over money, Granddad Harris used inheritance as a weapon, and he was prejudiced against Ed and Gat. The four Liars set fire to Clairmont, the family's house, to "change an evil". The fire went wrong. Gat, Johnny and Mirren died. Cadence survived with burns. The three Liars she spends the summer with are ghosts.

Title: the Liars lie to themselves and others; the family lies about being perfect; Cadence's mind lies to protect her.

2. How the accident happened and the outcome

What Cadence believes for most of the book

In late July of summer fifteen she swam alone at the tiny beach in her underwear, hit her head on a rock, and Mummy found her curled up in the water. She had hypothermia and a suspected head injury, and no clothes were found.

What really happened (the build-up)

  • Granddad's wife Tipper had died. The three aunts fought over her things, the Boston house, Windemere and the inheritance, and Granddad fuelled it (for example by threatening to leave his money to Harvard).
  • Granddad was prejudiced against Ed and Gat. He threatened Carrie's inheritance if she married Ed and threatened Johnny's college fund.
  • Cadence and Gat were told by Mummy and others to stay apart. After a violent lunch, everyone left the island and the four Liars drank the aunts' wine.
  • They decided to burn Clairmont as a symbol of greed, control, racism and "the patriarchy", hoping the family would stop fighting. They would call the fire departments afterwards.

The night

  • Each Liar took a floor: Gat the basement, Cadence the ground floor, Johnny the second, Mirren the top. They poured gas and used paper and newspaper as kindling.
  • They had no agreed time to light. Cadence soaked the study with books and lit it; it burned fastest. Johnny screamed upstairs and the back door stuck.
  • She fled through flames, searched the boathouse and Cuddledown, and ran back. The basement stairs were a wall of fire and the upper stairs collapsed.
  • Granddad had locked the dogs Fatima and Prince Philip in an upstairs room, and the Liars forgot them.

3. The six themes from the whiteboard

For every theme: define it, name characters and events, give evidence, link to the whole novel.

1. Yearning for love

Idea: almost every character wants love and approval and does not get it, or does damaging things to get it.

  • Cadence ↔ Gat: first love; he gives her roses and notes. Gat's love is complicated by Raquel and by his place in the family.
  • Gat → family: he longs to belong but is treated as an outsider (Granddad never uses his name).
  • Aunts: compete for Granddad's love (and money); they are "little girls trying to get in good with Daddy".
  • Mirren: invents a boyfriend, Drake Loggerhead, because she wants someone to look at her the way Gat looks at Cadence.
  • Cadence's father leaves; Cadence wants her mother's and grandfather's love; Penny's love continues "in spite of" what Cadence did.

Link: the Liars burn the house partly to protect the love between them.

2. Desire for change

Idea: the Liars refuse to accept injustice and want to change the family; they act, but violently and without thinking it through.

  • Gat's motto "Do not accept an evil you can change" and Cadence's "Always do what you are afraid to do" drive the plan.
  • Cadence says "We changed an evil we saw in the world", but the result is death.
  • Her giveaway project and hair dye show another form of change: penance and an attempt to be someone else.
  • Contrast: Mirren says you must accept the world as it is.

Link: shows the danger of idealism without consequences.

3. Reality vs. imagination · Health vs. post-traumatic disorder

Idea: Cadence cannot tell what is real. Trauma makes her mind replace the fire with a swimming accident.

  • Narrator is unreliable: amnesia, fantasies (her father shooting her, bleeding, the witch with the goose, skull images) and fairy-tale variations.
  • Clues: strange lies about doughnuts, Liars avoiding the family and questions, vanishing when others appear, dogs "passed on".
  • Her migraines are real pain whose cause is emotional (post-traumatic headaches). At the end the Liars appear as ghosts she needed.

Link: the reader is as confused as Cadence until the truth in Part Four and Five.

4. Greed

Idea: money and possessions control the Sinclairs.

  • Granddad's inheritance threats; the aunts fight about jewellery (black pearls), houses, and tablecloths.
  • Ivory statues, Mummy's shopping, Granddad's expensive empty New Clairmont: possessions replace feelings.
  • Gat and Cadence notice the staff (Ginny, Paulo) and wealth gap.

Link: greed drives the aunts' fights, which cause the fire.

5. Grief and not dealing with it: avoidance

Idea: the family hides pain instead of facing it.

  • Mummy: "Be normal now"; silence as "protective coating". After Tipper's and Dad's departures the family "erases" them.
  • Nobody tells Cadence about the fire; doctors advise silence. Carrie wanders at night; Bess scrubs; the aunts drink and shop.
  • Cadence herself avoids: amnesia, giving things away, medication.

Link: avoidance keeps the family stuck and the truth hidden.

6. Racism

Idea: the white, wealthy family does not accept people of colour.

  • Granddad says "young man", not Gat; he calls Ed "sir"; Tipper's smile hardens when she meets Ed.
  • He threatens Carrie's inheritance if she marries Ed. Gat compares himself to Heathcliff in Wuthering Heights.
  • Bess blames Carrie for bringing Ed and Gat: "not one of us".

Link: one of the evils the Liars want to change.

Outcasts (whiteboard note)

  • Cadence's father (leaves because he cannot be a Sinclair any longer) and Ed, Gat's uncle, are the outcasts.
  • Ed's proposal: he proposed to Carrie, she said no, because of her inheritance, since Granddad would cut her off. This shows greed and racism together.

6. Text excerpts: what to know

Beginning (Chapters 1–3)

  • Ironic "Welcome to the beautiful Sinclair family": no one is "a criminal", "an addict" or "a failure" (hidden problems).
  • First-person, unreliable narrator; wealth shown (island, houses); short lines and repetition; dark humour ("suffer migraines… do not suffer fools").
  • Her father's departure as the shot to the chest: she describes emotions as physical injury. Mummy: "Be normal, now."

Middle (for example Chapters 38–39, 49–50)

  • The Liars' mottos (Gat: "Do not accept an evil you can change"; Cadence: "Always do what you are afraid to do"; Mirren: "Be a little kinder than you have to").
  • Gat says Granddad sees him as Heathcliff; Cadence learns about Ginny and Paulo (privilege).
  • Clues: lies about trips, Mirren's illness and the messy Cuddledown.

Ending (Chapters 80–87)

  • The official truth in a cold report voice (Ch. 80); the night itself (Ch. 81); grief and guilt (Ch. 82); ghosts' goodbye (Ch. 84).
  • Last chapter: the fairy tale becomes the family's story; tragedy is "not glamorous"; final lines return to Cadence's opening: "I endure."

How to analyse an extract

  1. Who speaks and when (narrator's knowledge)? 2. What happens and where in the plot? 3. Which techniques (repetition, fairy tale, imagery, unreliable narration, short lines)? 4. Which theme? 5. What does it show about a character or the family?
