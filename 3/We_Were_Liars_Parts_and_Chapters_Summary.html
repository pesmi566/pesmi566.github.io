We Were Liars: Part and Chapter Summaries

E. Lockhart · 87 chapters in 5 parts · Full spoilers

Written from the uploaded novel. Chapter numbers follow that edition; other editions may number differently.

How to read thisPart 1 WelcomePart 2 VermontPart 3 Summer SeventeenPart 4 Look, a FirePart 5 Truth
How to read this. Cadence narrates in the present (summer seventeen) and in flashbacks to earlier summers. Until Chapter 58 she believes she hit her head in a night-time swimming accident in summer fifteen. She also does not realise that Gat, Johnny and Mirren are dead. Where a summary says "Cadence believes" or "in fact", that marks the gap between her view and the truth, which is only revealed from Chapter 58 on.

Recurring structures: a fairy-tale "variation" (Chapters 16, 20, 40, 57, 65, 79, 87), Granddad's mottos, "Be normal now. Right now. Because you are. Because you can be.", and the falling image ("down to rocky rocky bottom").

Part One: Welcome

Chapters 1–15 · Background, the Liars, summer fifteen and the aftermath

Part summary. Cadence presents the Sinclairs as a perfect, wealthy family and introduces the Liars: herself, her cousins Johnny and Mirren, and Gat, nephew of Aunt Carrie's partner Ed. She and Gat fall in love in summer fifteen, the summer her father leaves. Then comes the "accident": she is found half-drowned on the beach, loses her memory and develops severe migraines. The part ends with Gat's silence, her isolation and the family's habit of acting normal.

Reader's view vs truth: the "swimming accident" is Cadence's false belief; the real event (the fire) is hidden.

  1. Ch 1An ironic address: "Welcome to the beautiful Sinclair family." No one is a criminal, an addict or a failure. Beneath the surface are divorce, dwindling money and pills, and someone "desperately in love". They summer on a private island off Massachusetts.
  2. Ch 2Cadence introduces herself: nearly eighteen, lives in Burlington, Vermont, with Mummy and dogs, suffers migraines "since my accident". In summer fifteen her father leaves. She imagines being shot in the chest, but Mummy tells her to be normal. They throw away his things, redecorate and go to Beechwood.
  3. Ch 3The family background: Harris and Tipper Sinclair, their three daughters Penny, Carrie and Bess, and the three houses built for them: Windemere (Penny), Red Gate (Carrie), Cuddledown (Bess). Cadence is the eldest grandchild and heiress, "probably".
  4. Ch 4Summer eight: Gat arrives with Carrie's partner Ed. Tipper's smile hardens at Ed's dark skin, and Granddad pats Gat's head. The four children run to the tiny beach, and Cadence writes their names in the sand. This is the start of the Liars.
  5. Ch 5Summer fourteen: Cadence and Gat take the boat out and swim in freezing water, then share a blanket. Gat admits she is pretty. They begin walking the island perimeter and writing on each other's hands. She does not yet call it love.
  6. Ch 6Summer fifteen: Cadence sees Gat in the Red Gate kitchen with a dried beach rose and realises she loves him. She then sees him mail it to someone else. She tears roses off a bush and throws them into the sea.
  7. Ch 7Johnny tells her about Gat's girlfriend Raquel. Cadence acts normal. At a beach picnic Gat talks about land ownership and privilege, is told to shut up, and wades into the sea. She follows and they reconcile.
  8. Ch 8Late at night Gat lies on the Windemere walkway with the five goldens (Bosh, Grendel, Poppy, Prince Philip, Fatima). They hold hands and talk about God. Gat has lost his faith after a trip to India. He lends her his olive jacket.
  9. Ch 9In the Windemere attic they sort her father's books. Gat says "I love you, Cady" and they share their first kiss.
  10. Ch 10Granddad finds them and tells Gat to "watch yourself, young man". He makes Cadence retell the Cracker Jack story, a favourite piece of Sinclair legend. Afterwards she finds Gat on the perimeter and kisses him.
  11. Ch 11Granny Tipper died eight months earlier. Clairmont feels empty. Cadence collapses with grief, and Mummy insists on normality: silence is a "protective coating over pain". The family erases the dead and absent, but Gat mentions them freely. Cadence's pain appears as bleeding, and Gat bandages her wrists. Their romance continues secretly.
  12. Ch 12Late July: Cadence swims alone at the tiny beach in her underwear. Her clothes are never found. She believes she hit her head on a rock. Mummy finds her on the sand and she is taken to a Vineyard hospital. Gat never contacts her afterwards.
  13. Ch 13Six weeks later the migraines begin. After scans and medication she is diagnosed with post-traumatic headaches. The pain is described in violent images.
  14. Ch 14Summer sixteen: her father takes her on a ten-week European trip while Granddad "redoes" Clairmont. She misses the Liars, writes cheerful emails and gets no replies.
  15. Ch 15A second "welcome" to the Sinclairs: outdoor exercise, prescription drugs, stiff upper lips. In Burlington Cadence is the "sickly daughter", a mystery because she is a Sinclair. She and Mummy quarrel and Mummy monitors her food.

Part Two: Vermont

Chapters 16–22 · Winter and spring in Burlington; the decision to return

Part summary. Back in Vermont, Cadence tells fairy tales about her family, gives her possessions away, and lists what she remembers (and the odd fragments she cannot place). Mummy reveals that Cadence has forgotten every explanation she has been given. Granddad visits, frail and confused. Cadence is allowed four weeks on Beechwood before visiting her father.

  1. Ch 16Fairy tale 1: a king with three daughters tests their love. The youngest says she loves him "as meat loves salt" and is banished. She returns and stays to care for a power-mad tyrant, unsure whether it is love or the promised kingdom. This mirrors Cadence's family.
  2. Ch 17Cadence's giveaway project: one possession a day. She gives away a pillow, King Lear, a photo of Gran. She criticises her mother's materialism and the family's love of beautiful objects.
  3. Ch 18Amnesia: she lists what she remembers from summer fifteen and the fragments that make no sense (Mirren's hand with a jug of gas, "The black pearls?", Johnny's running feet, Granddad lit by a "bonfire", four Liars laughing). Mummy explains Cadence asked every day and forgot every answer, so she stopped asking.
  4. Ch 19Her father wants to take her to Australia; she wants Beechwood and the truth about Gat. Granddad visits Burlington, thin and confused, picking peonies and repeating Sinclair mottos.
  5. Ch 20Fairy tale 2: a king sends his three daughters to a dragon that eats them. "Who killed the girls? The dragon or their father?" The Australia trip is cancelled and Cadence gets four weeks on Beechwood, with the aunts warned that she is "fragile".
  6. Ch 21She packs almost nothing and gives away more, but keeps Gat's olive jacket, the last link to him. She tells herself she no longer loves him.
  7. Ch 22Taft phones the night before, asks if she is a drug addict and says Cuddledown is haunted. She reassures him. (A small early hint of the island's ghosts.)

Part Three: Summer Seventeen

Chapters 23–57 · Cadence returns and tries to piece the past together

Part summary. Cadence finds Clairmont replaced by a cold modern house. She reunites with the Liars at Cuddledown, but they behave oddly: they avoid the family, lie about their activities and dodge her questions. She recovers memories of money fights, Granddad's power over the aunts and his racism. She and Gat reconcile. Clues accumulate: unexplained lies, dead dogs, Carrie's night wandering, the Liars' mess and illness, ending with a flash of memory of fire.

Reader's view vs truth: the Liars appear alive and ordinary; in fact they are ghosts (revealed in Part Five).

  1. Ch 23Cadence and Mummy reach Woods Hole and cross by boat with Carrie. Cadence sees "New Clairmont", a glass-and-iron house where the Victorian house and the old maple stood. She nearly faints at the thought of the tree. Mummy: "Be normal now." The Liars wave from the fence.
  2. Ch 24Taft, Will and the twins meet her at the dock. The littles have moved to New Clairmont with Granddad, who now calls people "Mirren" by mistake. Bonnie says Cadence looks like a vampire.
  3. Ch 25The Liars reunite at Cuddledown. Cadence is angry but happy, and Gat flirts. She is embarrassed not to be at college. Mirren invents a boyfriend, Drake Loggerhead. The three Liars refuse to eat at New Clairmont, a plan made before Cadence arrived.
  4. Ch 26On the tiny beach Gat makes a joke about "troll feet" and says he thought he would never see her again. They hold hands.
  5. Ch 27In her Windemere room Cadence takes down pictures and books to give away. Mummy argues she is erasing herself just as Mummy erased Dad. Mummy insists she come to supper.
  6. Ch 28Cadence writes her memories on graph paper with questions (Where were my clothes? Was it really an injury from the swim? Did Gat leave her?). A migraine, described as a witch with an ivory goose, keeps her from supper.
  7. Ch 29At night Cadence sees Aunt Carrie wandering, thin and sleepless, saying she cannot sleep without Ed.
  8. Ch 30At breakfast she asks where Fatima and Prince Philip are. Mummy and Granddad say they "passed on a while back". Granddad wants the old blond Cadence back.
  9. Ch 31Mirren tells Cadence that the Barbie she posted never arrived. She also describes her mother's compulsive cleaning and drinking. Mirren gives Cadence purple rocks. Johnny wants things; Mirren values sentimental items like the drawings of Gran's dogs.
  10. Ch 32Gat hugs Cadence and asks if she remembers a flat rock. She is furious about his two years of silence. He asks to "start over" after lunch.
  11. Ch 33Granddad shows her bare, expensive New Clairmont and says the old life is gone. He is confused, asks for "the young man", and snaps when told what is missing.
  12. Ch 34Chaotic lunch. Will and Taft refuse to discuss last summer because Penny told them not to. They also report that the twins found pills in Cadence's room.
  13. Ch 35The Liars redecorate Cuddledown. Johnny seems not to know Cadence's memory loss, but Mirren and Gat do; all the island knows. After a mock quarrel Cadence and Gat "start over".
  14. Ch 36They play tennis and talk about Europe. Mirren says Cadence's stories about the Italian toilet are self-pity. Gat and Johnny say they have never been to Rome.
  15. Ch 37Supper with the separated "children's table". A memory resurfaces of an early July dinner when Granddad threatened to sell or renovate the Boston house, setting the aunts against each other. "I do not know what changed."
  16. Ch 38The Liars play Scrabble and swap mottos: Gat, "Do not accept an evil you can change"; Mirren, "Be a little kinder than you have to"; Cadence, "Always do what you are afraid to do."
  17. Ch 39Cadence kisses Gat, but he pulls away: "You don't know me." He names the staff, Ginny and Paulo, whom she has never learned, and says Granddad sees him as Heathcliff in Wuthering Heights.
  18. Ch 40Fairy tale 3: a tiny princess falls in love with a mouse (Gat). The kingdom rejects him, so they leave the palace. "If you want to live where people are not afraid of mice, you must give up living in palaces."
  19. Ch 41Migraine for two days. She lists the mysteries. Granddad takes her to Edgartown, buys fudge, introduces her to his lawyer Richard Thatcher and calls her "the first". He warns her not to tell Penny.
  20. Ch 42A memory of the ivory goose: Granddad boasts of buying ivory, and says "Don't take no for an answer". Cadence suggests donating it and he forbids her to tell him what to do with his money. Later he says, "A part of me died" with Tipper.
  21. Ch 43The Liars lie in the sun greased with baby oil, rejecting sunblock. Gat asks to start over again and she agrees to pretend. She gives him fudge.
  22. Ch 44Cadence and Mirren go to Edgartown. A memory returns of Gat giving her a Jaclyn Moriarty book inscribed "with everything, everything". Mirren dodges questions about why Gat vanished, then gets sick.
  23. Ch 45The Liars discover the Cuddledown roof and plan imaginary funerals, ashes and an Olympic handball party. Cadence thinks they will be young forever.
  24. Ch 46Cuddledown grows filthy. Mirren is ill. Johnny plays pointless games, Gat reads his hundred-novel list, and Cadence's longing triggers migraines. Broken bottles litter the porch.
  25. Ch 47Johnny says he disappeared because he is "a follower" and that Gat felt guilty about Raquel and hated himself.
  26. Ch 48Cadence gives Gat Charmed Life, inscribed "For Gat with everything, everything", and leaves it on his pillow.
  27. Ch 49On the roof Gat accuses her of seeking pity and reveals Granddad paid for the Europe trip. She admits the pain makes her wish she were dead. They reconcile and kiss.
  28. Ch 50Mirren admits Drake does not exist and asks Cadence to leave Gat alone because it will "end badly".
  29. Ch 51Third week: Cadence's pills run low. She catches the Liars lying about a doughnut shop. They have been looking at the Bible.
  30. Ch 52Gat visits her in her sick room and apologises about Raquel. He says he wants things he cannot have. Carrie wanders again while Will cries for her.
  31. Ch 53She gives Johnny a box of Legos; he refuses to build the tuna fish, and Will receives them gladly.
  32. Ch 54The Liars kayak to a rocky cliff. Mirren says Cadence should not come. Johnny and Gat jump; Cadence climbs up and jumps too, echoing "rocky rocky bottom". She wonders about parallel universes.
  33. Ch 55A memory of Carrie crying in Johnny's windbreaker. At Cuddledown Cadence asks Mirren what happened and guesses she was attacked. Mirren says only "a messed-up summer".
  34. Ch 56A tire swing hangs at Windemere. Cadence remembers the four Liars crammed in the Clairmont swing, spinning. In the tire is an envelope of dried beach roses from Gat.
  35. Ch 57Fairy tale 4: a mouse son, shamed by his mother, is sent away, and may one day return to "burn that fucking palace to the ground". It foreshadows the fire.

Part Four: Look, a Fire

Chapters 58–79 · The memory of the fire and what led to it

Part summary. Cadence remembers that the Liars burned Clairmont. Johnny and then her own memories explain why: the aunts' ruthless fights over inheritance, Granddad manipulating them, his racism towards Ed and Gat, and Gat and Cadence's fear of losing each other. The Liars decide to destroy the house as a symbol. Only then does Cadence recall that the dogs died, and, in Chapter 78, that her hands and feet were burned. The part ends with the witch fairy tale.

  1. Ch 58"Look. A fire." Cadence remembers: the four Liars set fire to Clairmont. She wakes in her bed and sees New Clairmont as a house "built on ashes". They burned not a home but "a symbol".
  2. Ch 59She tells Johnny, who explains the background: the aunts drank and fought, Granddad fuelled it, and Carrie pushed Johnny to compete for the money.
  3. Ch 60Memories of summer fifteen: Mummy made her write flattering letters to Granddad, and the aunts squabbled over Tipper's jewellery (the black pearls) and Bess demanded Mirren beg for tablecloths.
  4. Ch 61Cadence and Gat fall in love. At the twins' birthday dinner Granddad floats leaving his estate to Harvard (the "Sinclair Center for Socialization and Snacks"), which Cadence realises is a threat. The sisters' financial dependence is explained.
  5. Ch 62At cocktail hour Granddad pressures Penny over Windemere. Cadence is expected to flatter him. She refuses and says the house is too big.
  6. Ch 63Mummy threatens to send Cadence to her father. Cadence lies to Granddad, who then promises Windemere to Bess.
  7. Ch 64Gat points out that Granddad never says his name, calls Ed "sir", and threatens Carrie's inheritance if she marries Ed. He calls Harris and Tipper racist. Cadence sees the attic interruption as a threat.
  8. Ch 65Fairy tale 5: Beauty and the Beast, where the father can never see that the "beast" is human, like Granddad and Ed.
  9. Ch 66In the pantry Cadence and Gat overhear the aunts' drunken row. Bess accuses Carrie of bringing Ed and Gat; Carrie slaps Bess.
  10. Ch 67Granddad threatens Johnny's college fund to make him interfere with Ed. Gat is told to stay away from meals. Mummy orders Cadence to end things. The Liars smash the family's ivory statues to powder.
  11. Ch 68"We thought. We talked." The Liars imagine God striking Clairmont with lightning to punish the greedy and prejudiced so the family could be rebuilt. This is where the idea of burning the house takes shape. It is a mix of punishment and purification.
  12. Ch 69At a Clairmont lunch Penny pleads drunkenly; Granddad gives a speech about Sinclair values and says he will no longer reward them. Penny throws her wine glass.
  13. Ch 70Johnny recounts that day: family left the island, the Liars drank the aunts' wine. Gat feared losing the family. They called Clairmont "the seat of the patriarchy" and the fire a "purification". Cadence: "We burned that palace."
  14. Ch 71Cadence and Gat cry on the tiny beach. They talk themselves into action: "do what you are afraid to do". Gat says she wants to play God. Together they persuade Mirren and Johnny.
  15. Ch 72The plan: spread gas and light each floor, call the fire departments later. Cadence feels like a visionary: "We changed an evil we saw in the world." Mirren doubts it, Gat suspects New Clairmont is Granddad's self-punishment and hints that Cadence's giveaways are penance.
  16. Ch 73Cadence asks Mummy why no one told her about the fire. Mummy cites doctors and admits she was unsure Cadence could handle it.
  17. Ch 74Mirren admits she never read Cadence's emails and reads all twenty-eight, including a desperate one in which Cadence wrote she sometimes wants to die.
  18. Ch 75Typing her memories, Cadence realises Fatima and Prince Philip died: Granddad had shut them in an upstairs room, and the Liars forgot. She blames herself.
  19. Ch 76She runs out sobbing, imagining the dogs' death and realising her "bravado" was delusional.
  20. Ch 77Gat comforts her. When she asks what else she is missing, he says there is more but cannot say it, apologises for everything and leaves.
  21. Ch 78A memory of the hospital: blue sheets, Mummy crying, Granddad with fudge. Cadence's hands and feet are bandaged because they were burned.
  22. Ch 79Fairy tale 6: a witch curses three children, then gives them matches and says fire will cleanse their souls. She is the false friend whose ideas destroy them (Cadence's guilt).

Part Five: Truth

Chapters 80–87 · The facts, the night itself and the goodbye

Part summary. The official version is told: Gat, Mirren and Johnny died in a fire. Cadence recovers the full memory of the night and understands that the Liars she has been with all summer are ghosts, there because she needed them. They say goodbye and walk into the sea. Cadence begins to accept the loss, repairs her relationships and cleans Cuddledown. The last chapter retells the king and daughters story as the real family tragedy, with Cadence declaring she will endure.

  1. Ch 80"The truth as Granddad knows it": Gat, Mirren and Johnny died in a fire blamed on spilled boat fuel. Cadence was burned trying to rescue them and was found on the tiny beach. Granddad refused an investigation. Doctors treated her amnesia as grief and guilt, and the family rebuilt on the ashes.
  2. Ch 81The night itself: after drinking the aunts' wine, each Liar takes a floor (Gat basement, Cadence ground floor, Johnny second, Mirren top). Cadence lights the study, which burns fastest. Johnny screams and the back door sticks. She escapes through flames, searches the boathouse and Cuddledown, and returns. The stairwell collapses; there is "nowhere now to go but down".
  3. Ch 82On the Windemere steps Cadence takes in her guilt and mourns each Liar, imagining their burning. She realises they have been with her only as ghosts ("We have been here, this summer. And we have not been here."): "I needed them."
  4. Ch 83Mummy knocks. Cadence realises that her mother's "I love you" means "in spite of what I suspect you have done". She declines to come out and Mummy says she never needs to say sorry.
  5. Ch 84At Cuddledown the Liars say they cannot stay. Johnny wants to rest. Mirren apologises for lying. They walk to the tiny beach and Gat kisses her one last time. They wade into the sea and vanish, leaving Cadence alone.
  6. Ch 85Cadence sleeps for days. Her hair has faded back to brown. She joins the family, writes "Be a little. Kinder." on her hands, and Taft gives her a purple rock.
  7. Ch 86She goes shopping with Granddad, who still dislikes Ed but tolerates him. She comforts Taft, cleans Cuddledown and draws the Liars on the fridge.
  8. Ch 87The final fairy tale: the children died in a fire and the family survives; tragedy becomes "glamour" to outsiders, but the survivors know it is "ugly and tangled". Cadence names herself as perpetrator of a "foolish, deluded crime that became a tragedy", but says there will be more to her. She closes: "I endure."
